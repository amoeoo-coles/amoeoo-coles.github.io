Route and highways

Suggested route: I-11 / US-93 / US-95 south past Boulder City and the Hoover Dam bypass to Kingman; US-93 to Wickenburg; US-60 into the Phoenix area; then I-10 east to Tucson. OSRM’s named roads include I-11, US-93, US-95, I-40, US-60, I-17 and I-10.

Maps: Google Maps · OpenStreetMap / OSRM

Distance and time

OSRM (21 Nov 2026) for Las Vegas to Phoenix is 285.8 miles / about 6 hours, Phoenix to Tucson is 113.1 miles / about 2 hours 3 minutes, and the combined path is 398.9 miles and about 8 hours 3 minutes. Other published figures for Las Vegas–Tucson include Travelmath 417 miles and Flagma 406 miles / 8 hours 2 minutes.

Leg notes

Phoenix traffic can add time, especially on I-10 through the metro. Sites in the Phoenix area (Tempe) and at Eloy are large; confirm stall counts on Tesla’s official map before you travel.

Notable stops and attractions

The suggested route uses the Hoover Dam bypass on US-93 / I-11. The dam itself is a short, official visitor stop: Hoover Dam (Bureau of Reclamation). Tour hours, security rules, parking and visit length are not collected. This America Driving leg is routed via Phoenix on US-93 / US-60 / I-10, not via Flagstaff.

January weather (climate normals)

January on this leg is much milder than the Idaho and Sierra legs. Las Vegas, Kingman, Phoenix and Tucson all have normal January maxima in the mid-50s to high 60s °F. Kingman’s normal minimum is still a cool 30.9°F. This is not the Flagstaff-snow route used on the separate Las Vegas to Boca Chica page.

EV charging notes

Tesla Supercharger sites already listed on this page come from supercharge.info (retrieved 21–22 Nov 2026, as cited in the Supercharger table) and should be confirmed on Tesla Find Us. For other networks and connector types (including CCS), use the U.S. Department of Energy Alternative Fueling Station Locator (AFDC). A live count of non-Tesla stations in each town is not collected; the locator is the official source to check before you travel.

Supercharger coverage on this corridor is frequent in the cited dataset, including large sites at Boulder City, Kingman, Wickenburg, Tempe, Eloy and Tucson. Confirm stall counts on Tesla Find Us. Phoenix metro traffic, not a charging gap, is the main timing risk.

Border Patrol checkpoints

No official U.S. Border Patrol interior checkpoint on this measured Las Vegas–Phoenix–Tucson route was collected. This path stays on US-93 / US-60 / I-10 via Phoenix; it is not I-19 to Nogales or I-8. Absence from this page is not collected, not a guarantee.

SWOT analysis

FactorPoint
StrengthsFrequent Superchargers and a milder January climate than the northern legs (Phoenix normal max 67.6°F; Tucson 66.5°F).
StrengthsThe Hoover Dam bypass is on the suggested highway; the dam is an official, well-documented stop if you want it.
WeaknessesAbout 8 hours 3 minutes of driving, with Phoenix traffic able to add time on I-10.
WeaknessesA one-day Las Vegas–Tucson run leaves little slack for a dam visit plus metro congestion.
OpportunitiesA planned stop at Hoover Dam on the way out of the Las Vegas valley, if security and parking time are budgeted.
OpportunitiesOvernighting in Phoenix splits the day and avoids a late Tucson arrival after rush hour.
ThreatsPhoenix I-10 congestion, especially if the day also includes a Hoover Dam stop.
ThreatsWind, dust or unusual winter rain in the desert are possible, but station-level event frequencies for those hazards are not collected on this page.

Grouping these points into strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats is a qualitative judgement on this page, not an official travel advisory.
