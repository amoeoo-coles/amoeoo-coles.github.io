America Driving

v7 · 23 Nov 2026, 9:15 AM AEDT

A four-leg road trip from San Francisco International Airport (SFO) to Los Angeles, with Idaho’s endpoint set as Boise (that choice is stated here because “Idaho” alone is a large state). The legs are: SFO to Boise; Boise to Las Vegas; Las Vegas to Tucson via Phoenix; and Tucson to Los Angeles. Distances and drive times below are from a real routing source (OSRM on OpenStreetMap, retrieved 21 Nov 2026) and are compared with other published figures where they differ. They are driving time only — they do not include Supercharging, meals, traffic, weather or overnight stops. Supercharger stops are open sites listed on supercharge.info along each route; confirm them on Tesla’s official map at tesla.com/findus before you travel. Prices are included only when a source published one; otherwise this page says “check current price”.

A second, separate trip is also filed here: Las Vegas to Boca Chica (Starbase), routed via Flagstaff, at 1,680.9 mi and about 29 hours 52 min of driving (OSRM, 22 Nov 2026).

Leg 1 — San Francisco (SFO) to Idaho (Boise)

Open the Leg 1 subpage →

Idaho’s endpoint on this page is Boise. OSRM (21 Nov 2026) for SFO to Boise is 650.5 miles and about 12 hours 5 minutes of driving. That matches Air Miles Calculator’s published driving figure of 650.1 miles and 12 hours 5 minutes. Travelmath publishes 648 miles and 9 hours 58 minutes at a constant 65 mph — a shorter time because it does not reflect typical highway speed variation. Plan a long day, or split the drive, especially in winter over the Sierra Nevada (I-80).

Suggested route: US-101 / I-80 east from the Bay Area through Sacramento, Truckee and Reno; continue on I-80 to Fernley / Winnemucca; then US-95 north through McDermitt and Jordan Valley, Oregon; join I-84 / ID-55 into Boise. OSRM’s named roads for this path include I-80, US-95, ID-55 and I-84 / US-30 / I-184.

Maps: Google Maps · OpenStreetMap / OSRM

Leg 2 — Idaho (Boise) to Las Vegas

Open the Leg 2 subpage →

OSRM (21 Nov 2026) for Boise to Las Vegas is 622.7 miles and about 11 hours 50 minutes of driving. That is the shorter US-93 corridor (via Twin Falls, Wells and Ely), not the longer interstate loop through Salt Lake City. Published guides also put the direct route around 620–630 miles.

Suggested route (direct): I-84 east toward Twin Falls, then US-93 south through Jackpot, Wells and Ely, then US-6 / SR-318 to I-15 / US-93 into Las Vegas. OSRM’s named roads include I-84, US-93, US-50, US-6, SR-318 and I-15.

Alternate for charging coverage: I-84 to I-15 south via Salt Lake City, Beaver, Cedar City, St. George and Mesquite. OSRM via Salt Lake City is 760.2 miles and about 12 hours 43 minutes — longer, but Supercharger coverage on I-15 is denser (see the gap note below).

Charging gap (direct route): On 21 Nov 2026, supercharge.info listed no open Supercharger on the US-93 / SR-318 corridor between Ely and Las Vegas (about 240 miles of driving on the OSRM path). Pioche, Caliente, Alamo and similar towns in that gap did not have an open Supercharger in that dataset. This is the tightest range-planning problem on the trip. Use the in-car trip planner, do not arrive at Ely with a thin reserve, and check live conditions. If the car’s usable highway range in the expected weather would not cover Ely to Las Vegas with a buffer, take the I-15 alternate instead. Open I-15-area Superchargers include Salt Lake City, Beaver, Cedar City, St. George, Mesquite and Moapa (all listed as open on supercharge.info on 21 Nov 2026).

Leg 3 — Las Vegas to Tucson via Phoenix

Open the Leg 3 subpage →

This leg is routed via Phoenix, as specified. OSRM (21 Nov 2026) for Las Vegas to Phoenix is 285.8 miles / about 6 hours, Phoenix to Tucson is 113.1 miles / about 2 hours 3 minutes, and the combined path is 398.9 miles and about 8 hours 3 minutes. Other published figures for Las Vegas–Tucson include Travelmath 417 miles and Flagma 406 miles / 8 hours 2 minutes. Phoenix traffic can add time.

Suggested route: I-11 / US-93 / US-95 south past Boulder City and the Hoover Dam bypass to Kingman; US-93 to Wickenburg; US-60 into the Phoenix area; then I-10 east to Tucson. OSRM’s named roads include I-11, US-93, US-95, I-40, US-60, I-17 and I-10.

Leg 4 — Tucson to Los Angeles

Open the Leg 4 subpage →

OSRM (21 Nov 2026) for Tucson to downtown Los Angeles is 485.9 miles and about 8 hours 39 minutes. If the drop-off is Los Angeles International Airport (LAX), OSRM is 503.0 miles and about 9 hours 2 minutes. Other published I-10 figures include about 470–488 miles and roughly 7–9 hours depending on traffic and the exact endpoints. The main I-10 west route goes back through the Phoenix metro after you have already passed Phoenix on leg 3 — that is how I-10 is laid out, not a second sightseeing loop.

Suggested route: I-10 west from Tucson through the Phoenix area, Quartzsite and Ehrenberg, across the Colorado River at Blythe, then through Indio, Beaumont and Ontario into Los Angeles (continue to I-105 / local roads for LAX). OSRM’s named roads include I-10 (and I-10 Express Lanes in the LA basin), I-605, I-105 and CA-1 near the coast.

Hertz and other Tesla rental options

Hertz’s Tesla rental page advertises Tesla rentals at selected US locations, including San Francisco SFO and Los Angeles LAX (also Las Vegas, Phoenix and others). The page highlights the Tesla Model 3. Model availability is not guaranteed at both airports on your dates — check the reservation form. Check current price; the marketing page did not publish a rate that can be cited here.

One-way caveat (important): Hertz’s published one-way rental terms for SFO state: “Tesla reservations are not allowed to return one way.” Source: Hertz rental terms, SFO, One-Way Rentals (retrieved 21 Nov 2026). The same sentence appears on the Phoenix one-way terms page. So the SFO-pickup / LAX-drop-off plan may not be available on a Hertz Tesla. Confirm this at booking. If one-way is refused, options are a round-trip return to SFO (which does not match this itinerary), a non-Tesla one-way rental (intercity fees may apply — check current price), or another operator.

Other companies and peer-to-peer platforms sometimes list Teslas, including at airports. Availability, one-way permission and fees vary by listing. This page does not invent those prices: check current price and the one-way / drop-off rules before you rely on them.

Supercharging billing (Hertz EV terms)

Hertz’s electric-vehicle rental terms (retrieved 21 Nov 2026 from the LAX Tesla terms page) say:

  • If you use a Tesla Supercharger, the charging cost is billed back to Hertz and added to the rental with a 2.8% convenience fee. Charges may appear after the final invoice.
  • Hertz says the battery charging limit on a Tesla should be set at 90% maximum.
  • You may incur an idle fee if the car stays in a stall after charging finishes; the renter is responsible.
  • You are never required to return the vehicle with more than 75% battery charge.
  • Published range figures are not guaranteed. Weather, driving and road conditions affect range; it is the renter’s responsibility to keep enough charge to reach a station or return the car.

Tesla’s own Supercharger pricing, if you rent outside Hertz, is shown in the car and on Tesla’s site at the time of charging. Check current price — this page does not invent a per-kWh rate.

Range planning tips

  • Use the in-car trip planner and the live map on tesla.com/findus; stalls, power and opening status change.
  • Treat the Ely to Las Vegas stretch on the direct US-93 route as the main risk. If usable highway range plus a reserve will not cover that gap in the expected weather and wind, use the I-15 alternate.
  • Winter driving over the Sierra (leg 1) and high desert (legs 1–2) reduces range. Do not plan to arrive at a Supercharger at 0%.
  • Arrive with a buffer at smaller sites (some towns have 8 stalls; they can fill). Quartzsite and several California I-10 sites are much larger.
  • Allow time for charging in the day plan — the OSRM times above are driving only.
  • If the hire car is a Hertz Tesla, set the charge limit to 90% as Hertz asks, and move off the stall when charging ends to avoid idle fees.

SWOT analysis

This SWOT covers the four-leg SFO–Boise–Las Vegas–Tucson–Los Angeles trip. The separate Las Vegas to Boca Chica trip has its own analysis.

FactorPoint
StrengthsThe four legs are fully routed with OSRM distances and named Supercharger towns. Legs 3 and 4 have frequent chargers and a mild January climate.
StrengthsOfficial 511 / traveler-information sites exist in every state on the route, and there is a documented I-15 alternate for the leg 2 charging gap.
WeaknessesLegs 1 and 2 are each about 12 and 11 hours 50 minutes of driving. Stacking them without overnight splits is a fatigue problem, made worse by Sierra snow and the Ely–Las Vegas gap.
WeaknessesTravel dates, hotels and some hire-fee details are not collected on this hub.
OpportunitiesSplit leg 1 at Reno and leg 2 at Ely or via I-15; use Hoover Dam, Saguaro or an optional Joshua Tree stop only when the day is already short.
OpportunitiesThe separate Las Vegas to Boca Chica page can follow a Las Vegas stay, including after CES 2027, if dates are later set.
ThreatsJanuary snow on I-80 (Truckee / Donner normals around 40–44 inches) and a cold, Supercharger-gapped US-93 south of Ely.
ThreatsFatigue, winter range loss, and Los Angeles traffic at the end of the trip.

Grouping these points into strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats is a qualitative judgement on this page, not an official travel advisory.
