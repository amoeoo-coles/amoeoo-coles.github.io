Space Shuttles 3S

v1 · 24 Nov 2026, 9:15 PM AEDT

Three NASA space shuttle orbiters are on public display at the museums below: Discovery at the Smithsonian’s Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center, Atlantis at Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex, and Enterprise at the Intrepid Sea, Air & Space Museum. This page checks locations, admission and display details against each museum’s official site and against NASA and Smithsonian records. Prices and hours were read from those official pages on 24 Nov 2026, 9:15 PM AEDT (Melbourne time), and can change.

Discovery at the Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center

Washington, D.C. area · Chantilly, Virginia

The Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum’s companion facility is the Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center at 14390 Air and Space Museum Parkway, Chantilly, VA 20151. Official directions say the museum is near Dulles Airport.12

Admission is free and no passes are required. The center is open daily from 10:00 am to 5:30 pm and is closed on 25 December. Official directions state a parking fee of $15 before 4 pm. Buses, and cars that stay less than 30 minutes, are not charged. Parking is free for all buses. The official directions page checked for this version does not state a price after 4 pm; that figure is not collected.12

Space Shuttle Discovery (OV-103) is on display in the James S. McDonnell Space Hangar. The Smithsonian object record says Discovery was the third orbiter to fly in space, entered service in 1984, and retired as the oldest and most utilized orbiter. It flew 39 Earth-orbital missions, spent a total of 365 days in space, and traveled almost 240 million kilometers (150 million miles)—more than the other orbiters. The same record says Discovery “is preserved as intact as possible as it last flew in 2011 on the 133rd Space Shuttle mission,” and that NASA transferred it to the Smithsonian in April 2012.34

The museum has two locations. Discovery is at the Udvar-Hazy Center, not at the National Air and Space Museum building on the National Mall in Washington, D.C. The official visit page notes that free timed-entry passes are required for the museum in D.C., and that no passes are required at Udvar-Hazy.143

The source table described Discovery as displayed with its landing gear down in untouched post-flight condition. The official object record does not use those exact words. An Air Force news article quotes Smithsonian curator Valerie Neal: “We asked NASA not to clean the exterior or repaint it,” and “We wanted Discovery to be as it was after it flew its last mission.” A 2014 collectSPACE interview with Neal describes the landing gear and tires from the March 2011 landing, with the orbiter supported on posts rather than sitting on the tires. Those two accounts are secondary to the official object record.1516

Atlantis at Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex

Florida · Merritt Island, east of Orlando

Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex is at Space Commerce Way, Merritt Island, FL 32953. The official travel page publishes GPS coordinates 28°30′56.0″N, 80°40′54″W, and describes the complex as “just one small step from Orlando and Daytona Beach.” Official driving directions from Orlando are approximately 50 miles on State Road 50 or State Road 528. The source table said “about 1 hour east of Orlando”; a drive time is not published on the official travel page checked for this version, so that time is not collected. The same page warns drivers to search for “Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex,” not “Kennedy Space Center.”7

The visitor complex is operated for NASA by Delaware North and is entirely visitor-funded; the official About page says no U.S. tax dollars are used to provide visitor access. The same page states that it is a Smithsonian Affiliate.85

Admission is paid. On the official ticket store, checked on 24 Nov 2026, 1-day admission was $77.00 for adults ages 12+ and $67.00 for children ages 3–11. Military 1-day tickets were $72.00 (adult) and $62.00 (child). 2-day adult tickets were $91.00 and 2-day child tickets $81.00. The source table gave a range of about $75 to $83. The official adult 1-day price sits in that range; the official child 1-day price is below it.5

Parking is not included with daily admission. Official parking rates, collected at the entrance to the lot, are $5.00 for motorcycles, $15.00 for automobiles, and $20.00 for oversized vehicles, motor homes or RVs. Annual passholders receive complimentary parking with a valid pass.76

The official ticket store page checked for this version states that the complex is open 9 am to 5 pm. Confirm hours for the day you go, because the official site also notes that exhibits and tours may change for operational requirements or launch preparations.5

Space Shuttle Atlantis (OV-104) is included with daily admission. The official attraction page, updated 12 March 2025, says Atlantis completed 33 missions and logged more than 126 million miles and 307 days in space, and that its final mission was also the final Space Shuttle Program mission. Visitors “greet Atlantis on the top floor, seeing her suspended in space as only astronauts saw her: with the payload bay doors open and Canadarm fully extended. Her suspension is at a 43.21-degree angle, a lesser-known nod to a launch countdown.”6

Enterprise at the Intrepid Sea, Air & Space Museum

New York · west side of Manhattan, Pier 86

The Intrepid Museum’s official directions page says it “is located on the West side of Manhattan on Pier 86, 12th Avenue and 46th Street,” with the mailing address Pier 86, W 46th St and 12th Ave, New York, NY 10036-4103. The source table named Hell’s Kitchen; the official directions page does not use that neighborhood name.12

The Space Shuttle Pavilion, home to Enterprise, is aboard the retired aircraft carrier USS Intrepid. Official ticketing says general admission includes access to Intrepid, the Space Shuttle Pavilion, Submarine Growler and temporary exhibitions.910

Admission is paid. On the official ticketing page, checked on 24 Nov 2026, general admission was $38 for adults (ages 13–64), $36 for seniors (65+) and college students with valid ID, and $28 for children (ages 5–12). The source table’s figure of about $38 matches the official adult price. The official visitor information page says all members of a party, “excluding children 0–4,” must have an admission ticket, so children 0–4 do not need a ticket. A dollar amount for that age group was not shown as a figure on the ticketing page extract used for this version. NYC residents receive 50% off general admission with qualifying identification; U.S. military and veterans receive free admission with valid identification.911

Official hours: 1 October to 31 March, 10:00 am to 5:00 pm daily, with last entry at 4:00 pm. 1 April to 30 September, 10:00 am to 5:00 pm Monday to Friday (last entry 4:00 pm) and 10:00 am to 6:00 pm on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays (last entry 5:00 pm). The museum is closed on Thanksgiving and Christmas Day. It does not have a visitor parking lot and is not affiliated with any parking garage.1112

Space Shuttle Enterprise (OV-101) is NASA’s prototype orbiter. A NASA history article states that OV-101 was “the first Space Shuttle vehicle designed not to fly in space but to be used in ground and aerial tests within the Earth’s atmosphere.” In 1977 it carried out the Approach and Landing Tests at Dryden, including five independent flights after release from the Shuttle Carrier Aircraft, the first of them on 12 August 1977. The Intrepid Museum’s collection record says Enterprise was constructed without engines or a functional heat shield, and that those five flights, between August and October 1977, proved that the orbiters could land on a runway. NASA transferred Enterprise from the Udvar-Hazy Center to the Intrepid Museum in 2012; the pavilion opened to the public on 19 July 2012.131710

Verified against the source table

  • Udvar-Hazy admission and parking. The source table’s “free admission with paid parking” matches the official site. The official parking figure checked for this version is $15 before 4 pm.
  • Discovery’s 39 missions, and the National Mall. Both match the Smithsonian object record and the official visit page. The official wording for the orbiter’s condition is “preserved as intact as possible as it last flew in 2011,” not the source table’s “landing gear down in untouched post-flight condition.” See the Discovery card for the curator quotations.
  • Kennedy Space Center ticket price. The source table said about $75 to $83. The official 1-day adult price on 24 Nov 2026 was $77, which sits in that range. The official 1-day child price was $67, which is below that range. Parking is a separate $15 automobile fee.
  • Atlantis display. 33 missions, a 43.21-degree angle, open payload bay doors and extended Canadarm all match the official attraction page. Smithsonian Affiliate status and operation for NASA by Delaware North match the official About page.
  • Drive from Orlando. The source table said about one hour east of Orlando. Official directions give about 50 miles from Orlando and do not publish a drive time; that time is not collected.
  • Intrepid adult price. The source table’s about $38 matches the official adult general admission of $38 on 24 Nov 2026. The Space Shuttle Pavilion is included.
  • Enterprise history. Prototype / test orbiter, approach and landing tests, and “designed not to fly in space” match NASA and the Intrepid collection record.
  • Neighborhood wording. The source table placed Intrepid in Hell’s Kitchen. The official directions page says “the West side of Manhattan on Pier 86” and does not use that neighborhood name.
