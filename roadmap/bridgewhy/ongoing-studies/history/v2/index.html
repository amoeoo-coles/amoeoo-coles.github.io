<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/aci/">Cisco ACI</a>14Cisco ACI Training from ScratchmatchingThis is the matching course for the ACI track: the curriculum lists it as Cisco ACI Training from Scratch (the course page itself is now titled ACI from Scratch), with live classes and hands-on lab videos, later expanded with a long lesson list, shared services and multi-tenant topologies. You already automate ACI, so skip first-contact “what is a tenant or EPG” material. Focus on bridge-domain forwarding, contracts, L3Out, shared services, and any lab you can reproduce from Ansible or Terraform rather than the GUI.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/aci/">Cisco ACI</a>13Data Center VXLAN with BGP-EVPNrelatedCovers modern data-centre VXLAN overlays, underlay MTU, BUM handling, MP-BGP EVPN and external connectivity, with EVE-NG labs. The useful work for this track is the contrast with ACI’s IS-IS, COOP and VXLAN control plane. Focus on EVPN route types and how a leaf learns a remote endpoint, then write the ACI equivalent. Skip a generic “what is VXLAN” recap if that is already solid.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/aci/">Cisco ACI</a>9IS-IS Routing ProtocolrelatedCovers IS-IS as a link-state protocol: NSAP addressing, levels and packet analysis. ACI’s fabric underlay is IS-IS, so this is the protocol view of faults that otherwise show up as a silent TEP or COOP problem. Focus on flooding, levels and LSDB symptoms that would stop a leaf learning remote endpoints. Skip deep NSAP tutorial work unless you are also studying service-provider IS-IS.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/aci/">Cisco ACI</a>21Network Automation (Python, YANG, RESTCONF & Ansible)relatedCovers Python for network engineers, JSON and YANG, RESTCONF and Ansible. That is the tooling layer for the ACI automation you already run (cisco.aci, the APIC REST API, and a desired state in git). Focus on idempotent Ansible, structured data and REST patterns you can point at the APIC. Skip introductory Python syntax if you already write the tooling.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/bgp/">BGP</a>10BGP, MPLS & Enterprise Network DesignmatchingThis is the matching course for the BGP track: advanced dynamic routing, ISP and enterprise WAN architecture, BGP, MPLS Layer-3 VPNs, a design module and EVE-NG labs (Modules D–G). Focus on policy (communities, route reflection, add-path) and the design module, at the depth this study page is aimed at. Use the MPLS VPN labs as a contrast with EVPN and ACI, and skip only the parts that repeat CCNA-level neighbour bring-up.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/bgp/">BGP</a>13Data Center VXLAN with BGP-EVPNrelatedCovers MP-BGP EVPN as the data-centre control plane, plus underlay MTU, BUM traffic and external connectivity. EVPN is the address family that sits next to classic IPv4 and IPv6 unicast on this BGP track, and it overlaps with both ACI and campus fabrics. Focus on route types 1–5 and route-target design. Skip underlay cabling walkthroughs if you already operate a leaf-spine.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/bgp/">BGP</a>7IPv6 FundamentalsrelatedThe IPv6 course already includes MP-BGP IPv6 and dual-stack or NAT64 transition. You will carry IPv6 unicast, and possibly IPv4 NLRI with an IPv6 next hop, on the same sessions this BGP track is building. Focus on the MP-BGP IPv6 and next-hop sections; leave addressing-plan and Router Advertisement labs for the IPv6 study page.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/bgp/">BGP</a>8OSPF Deep-DiverelatedCovers OSPF link-state internals, LSA types, multi-area design, stub areas and EVE-NG labs. BGP next-hop resolution and Prefix Independent Convergence still depend on a boringly healthy IGP. Focus on how IGP metric and next hop show up in the BGP decision process. Skip a full LSA-type recitation unless you want a refresh.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/bgp/">BGP</a>9IS-IS Routing ProtocolrelatedCovers IS-IS as the other common underlay IGP. Many of the fabrics and WANs you already operate — including the ACI underlay — use IS-IS rather than OSPF under BGP. Focus on levels and what a missing IS-IS adjacency does to BGP next hops. Skip this related course if you are not using IS-IS outside ACI.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/sdwan/">Cisco SD-WAN</a>15Cisco SD-WAN (Complete Architecture, Design & GCP Lab)matchingThis is the matching course for the SD-WAN track: enterprise architecture, vManage, vSmart and vBond, OMP, TLOCs, policies, Cloud OnRamp, and GCP or EVE-NG labs (Modules A–F). Focus on OMP versus BFD, TLOC colours, centralised versus localised policy, and the design and migration modules. Skip controller-install walkthroughs once you can draw the four planes and the bring-up sequence from memory.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/sdwan/">Cisco SD-WAN</a>11Enterprise Quality of Service (QoS)relatedCovers traffic classification, queuing, congestion management and WAN or DMVPN QoS policies. Application-aware routing and SLA classes in Catalyst SD-WAN are QoS policy with a controller in front. Focus on classification and WAN queuing that you can map onto AAR or a centralised data policy. Skip a first-principles “what is a DSCP” review if that is already solid.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/sdwan/">Cisco SD-WAN</a>10BGP, MPLS & Enterprise Network DesignrelatedCovers the classic BGP and MPLS WAN this overlay often rides on or replaces; MPLS is frequently a TLOC colour. The SD-WAN policy language is easier to read if you can still read a PE/CE design. Focus on the WAN design and L3VPN edge. Skip repeating the full BGP path-selection labs already covered on the BGP study page.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/sdwan/">Cisco SD-WAN</a>21Network Automation (Python, YANG, RESTCONF & Ansible)relatedCovers Python, YANG, RESTCONF and Ansible. Manager templates and device CLI should live in git and, where possible, be driven as code rather than click-ops. Focus on structured configuration and API patterns you can apply to Manager. Skip language basics if you are already fluent.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/ise/">Cisco ISE</a>19Cisco ISE (Identity Services Engine)matchingThis is the matching course for the ISE track: network access control, 802.1X, EAP-PEAP, MAB, RADIUS and packet-level troubleshooting. Focus on the authentication versus authorisation flow, the RADIUS attributes that actually drive the result, and the captures. The public course is lighter on TrustSec, pxGrid and the ERS or OpenAPI than this study checklist, so use the official ISE guides for those. Skip a first 802.1X switch-template if you have already built one.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/ise/">Cisco ISE</a>18Network Security FundamentalsrelatedCovers cryptography, PKI, TLS and IPsec, firewalls, Zero Trust and Cisco security products. EAP-TLS, posture and Zero Trust access all rest on PKI and TLS, which ISE policy assumes you already understand. Focus on PKI, TLS and Zero Trust. Skip a general firewall product tour unless you are integrating ISE with one.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/ise/">Cisco ISE</a>16Cisco SD-Access (Software-Defined Networking)relatedCovers the campus fabric, virtual networks and the border or fusion hand-off. SD-Access and ISE share scalable groups / SGTs, so this is the fabric side of the same policy language. Focus on how an ISE authorisation becomes an SGT or a VN in the fabric. Leave underlay and LAN-automation depth for the SD-Access study page.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/ise/">Cisco ISE</a>17Wireless Fundamentals & Cisco WLC-9800relatedCovers RF fundamentals, 802.11, CAPWAP, Catalyst 9800 deployment on EVE-NG, and wireless security. Wireless 802.1X and guest or SSID design are a large part of a production ISE deployment. Focus on the security, SSID and RADIUS-on-the-WLC parts. Skip RF theory unless you also own the wireless design.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/ise/">Cisco ISE</a>5Wireshark Packet AnalysisrelatedCovers practical capture, display filters, protocol troubleshooting and latency diagnostics. ISE problems are often won or lost in a RADIUS or EAP pcap, not in the GUI log alone. Focus on capture points and filters for RADIUS, EAP and CoA. Skip a first-time Wireshark tour if you already use it daily.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/ipv6/">IPv6</a>7IPv6 FundamentalsmatchingThis is the matching course for the IPv6 track: addressing, Neighbor Discovery, OSPFv3, MP-BGP IPv6, dual-stack and NAT64, with labs, captures and notes (Modules A–D). It already reaches MP-BGP, which is where a CCIE-level plan should spend time. Use the fundamentals modules as a rapid recap, then focus on first-hop security, OSPFv3 or MP-BGP, and IPv6-only or NAT64 failure modes. Skip rote “how many bits in an address” drills.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/ipv6/">IPv6</a>10BGP, MPLS & Enterprise Network DesignrelatedCovers the BGP policy and design that carry IPv6 as an address family, including the enterprise WAN edge. <a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/ipv6/">IPv6</a>8OSPF Deep-DiverelatedCovers OSPF LSDB synchronisation, LSA types, multi-area design and stub areas. OSPFv3 is the IGP the IPv6 fundamentals course introduces; this is the deeper area and LSDB view. Focus on OSPFv3 differences (no IPv4 LSA types, instance IDs). Skip IPv4-only area-type labs if you are here only for IPv6.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/ipv6/">IPv6</a>6TCP Protocol Deep-DiverelatedCovers the TCP lifecycle, flow and error control, MSS and MTU, congestion control and Wireshark analysis. IPv6 Path MTU Discovery and ICMPv6 Packet Too Big are the failure mode this course’s MTU module makes visible. Focus on MSS/MTU and what happens when Packet Too Big is filtered. Skip handshake and window basics if they are already solid.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/ipv6/">IPv6</a>5Wireshark Packet AnalysisrelatedCovers capture technique and display filters. For this track, that means Router Solicitation and Advertisement, Neighbour Solicitation and Advertisement, DHCPv6 and ICMPv6. Focus on those filters and on capture points that see first-hop attacks. Skip general capture setup if the ISE or TCP pages already covered it.

Focus on the control plane, VN and SGT policy, and the border/fusion module — that is what joins SD-Access to the WAN, to ISE and to ACI. <a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/sd-access/">Cisco SD-Access</a>12Campus Network Design from ScratchrelatedCovers enterprise campus architecture methodology, requirement gathering, and Layer-2/Layer-3 topologies for large scale. <a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/sd-access/">Cisco SD-Access</a>19Cisco ISE (Identity Services Engine)relatedCovers NAC, 802.1X and RADIUS, which is how users and devices actually receive a VN or an SGT in the fabric. Focus on authorisation results that the fabric can enforce, and on what happens when the PSN is unreachable (critical VLAN). Leave a full ISE policy-set redesign on the ISE study page.

SD-Access also uses VXLAN in the data plane; EVPN versus LISP is the control-plane contrast worth writing down. Focus on encapsulation and VNID versus SD-Access virtual networks. <a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/sd-access/">Cisco SD-Access</a>17Wireless Fundamentals & Cisco WLC-9800relatedCovers the Catalyst 9800 WLC, CAPWAP and wireless security. The WLC is a fabric role, and SSID-to-VN mapping sits on this course. Focus on how a WLC joins the fabric and where RADIUS or ISE sits. Skip RF planning unless you own it.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/sd-access/">Cisco SD-Access</a>15Cisco SD-WAN (Complete Architecture, Design & GCP Lab)relatedCovers Catalyst SD-WAN architecture and policy. SD-WAN transit is one of the documented ways to join an SD-Access fabric to the WAN. Focus on the transit hand-off and MTU. Leave OMP and TLOC depth on the SD-WAN study page.
<a href="/roadmap/bridgewhy/ongoing-studies/git/">Git</a>20Git for Network Engineers - EnglishmatchingThis is the matching course for the Git track: version control fundamentals tailored for network configurations and automation workflows, with video lessons and resources.
