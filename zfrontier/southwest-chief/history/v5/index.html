Southwest Chief

v5 · 24 Nov 2026, 12:37 PM AEDT

The Southwest Chief runs about 2,265 miles (roughly 3,645 km) from Chicago to Los Angeles, crossing eight states: Illinois, Iowa, Missouri, Kansas, Colorado, New Mexico, Arizona, and California. The full journey takes about 43 hours with two nights on board.

Eight states, two nights on board, about 43 hours Chicago to Los Angeles. The published route length is about 2,265 miles (roughly 3,645 km). The map line below is the OpenStreetMap rail geometry for Amtrak’s westbound Southwest Chief (train 3), not a road route and not a new mileage survey.

Does it go through the Colorado River?

Not really. It only crosses the Colorado River once, at Needles, California, right at the headwaters of Lake Havasu, and that happens overnight heading west. It does follow the Little Colorado River briefly near Holbrook, Arizona, which eventually joins the main Colorado at the Grand Canyon, but that is a short stretch, not a highlight.

What to watch for

  1. Raton Pass (Colorado/New Mexico border) — the crown jewel. The train climbs through the Sangre de Cristo Mountains on curves and tunnels that feel barely wider than the train itself, at the highest point on the whole route. Westbound train 3 crosses it in the morning in daylight all year.
  2. Glorieta Pass and Apache Canyon (New Mexico) — narrow, twisting canyon passages with red rock walls rising close to the train. One of the most photogenic stretches.
  3. High desert of New Mexico and Arizona — wide-open vistas, distant mesas, and the Sandia Mountains framing Albuquerque, especially strong at sunset on westbound departures. Albuquerque’s station stop is usually longer than most, giving you time to stretch your legs.
  4. Approaches to Flagstaff and the Colorado Plateau — San Francisco Peaks, ponderosa pine country, and the sense of approaching the Grand Canyon region.
  5. California desert and San Bernardino Mountains — the final western stretch offers desert scenery followed by the climb into the mountains before dropping into the Los Angeles basin. Joshua trees line the tracks through the Mojave.

Other notable stops

Dodge City (Boot Hill Museum, Wyatt Earp territory), Lamy (shuttle connection to Santa Fe), Gallup (best stop for side trips to the Four Corners region, Mesa Verde, Canyon de Chelly), Winslow (the Eagles “Take It Easy” statue and the restored La Posada Hotel), and Flagstaff (Thruway bus link to the Grand Canyon Railway in Williams).

Practical notes for November

Westbound (train 3) gives you Raton Pass and the New Mexico desert in full daylight all year — the better direction for scenery. But Winslow and Flagstaff will be dark from now until mid-March, so if the Grand Canyon matters, plan around the Thruway bus from Flagstaff to Williams. The Sightseer Lounge car is where you want to be parked for all of it.

SWOT analysis

A sourced look at riding the Southwest Chief, grouped into strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats. Click a column header to sort. Likelihood and impact of related risks are in the table below.

FactorPoint
StrengthsWestbound train 3 crosses Raton Pass and the New Mexico desert in daylight all year, and the Sightseer Lounge is the place to watch it from. That direction and seating note is already on this page. Wikipedia also lists a Sightseer lounge car among the train’s on-board facilities.
StrengthsThe train offers Superliner coach seats and private rooms (Roomette, Bedroom, Bedroom Suite, Family Room and Accessible Bedroom), Traditional Dining and Cafe Service, official on the Southwest Chief route page. Private-room fares include complimentary meals, a First Class attendant and a bed at night, per Amtrak’s private-room page.
StrengthsOfficial connections exist at two of the highlight stops: the RoadRunneR Shuttle meets trains 3 and 4 at Lamy for Santa Fe (passengers leaving Santa Fe should call the shuttle the day before), per the Amtrak Santa Fe (SAF) station page; and the route page advertises Thruway buses to the Grand Canyon Railway in Williams from Flagstaff.
WeaknessesOn-time performance is well below Amtrak’s 80% customer-OTP minimum for long-distance routes. In the Amtrak Host Railroad Report, data through August 2026, Southwest Chief customer OTP was 21.6% that month and 32.7% over the most recent 12 months. Late riders were 121 minutes late on average in August and 109 minutes over those 12 months. The report marks the figures as preliminary.
WeaknessesFreight train interference is the largest delay category for this train on BNSF in that August 2026 report: 629 minutes of freight-train-interference delay per 10,000 train-miles. Host-responsible delay was 1,246 minutes per 10,000 train-miles in August and 938 over 12 months, against a 900-minute target. Most of the Chicago–Los Angeles route is on BNSF.
WeaknessesWestbound, Winslow and Flagstaff are in the dark from November until mid-March, so the Colorado Plateau and the Grand Canyon approaches are not a daylight highlight in that season. Thruway times Flagstaff–Williams are not collected on this page.
OpportunitiesChoosing westbound train 3 still gives you Raton Pass and the New Mexico high desert in daylight in winter, even when Arizona is dark. A night in Flagstaff or Williams, using the Thruway to the Grand Canyon Railway, is the way this page already suggests if the Grand Canyon matters.
OpportunitiesA private room turns the two nights on board into a bed, with meals included, which matters on a 43-hour ride. Cafe service remains available to all customers on this route, per Amtrak’s Traditional Dining page.
ThreatsThe scenic Raton segment carries little freight. Wikipedia’s Raton Pass article records that in 2012 BNSF said it could not justify maintaining the line to Amtrak’s standards between La Junta, Colorado, and Lamy, New Mexico. A coalition later won repeated federal grants to keep the line in passenger condition, including a 2020 CRISI grant described in an Amtrak Media release, 23 Sep 2020. Long-term upkeep still depends on that kind of funding.
ThreatsRaton Pass is a steep, high railroad: grades of up to 3.5% and a tunnel at 7,588 ft (2,313 m), the highest point on the line, per Wikipedia’s Raton Pass article (the highway summit is separately listed at 7,834 ft). Weather-related delay is a defined third-party category in Amtrak’s host-railroad reports. A Chief-specific winter delay count is not collected here.
ThreatsWhen the train is late, tight connections at Lamy and Flagstaff are at risk. Build slack, and treat shuttle and Thruway times as something to confirm close to travel — current times are not collected on this page.

Grouping these points into strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats is a qualitative judgement on this page, not an official travel advisory.
