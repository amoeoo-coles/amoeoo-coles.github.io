SWChief train

v1 · 26 Nov 2026, 9:45 PM AEDT

Amtrak’s Southwest Chief is the daily Chicago–Los Angeles train — train 3 westbound and train 4 eastbound — about 2,265 miles (roughly 3,645 km) and about 43 hours, with two nights on board. This page is about the train itself: the typical Superliner consist (car order), where first-class sleepers, the dining car and the Sightseer Lounge sit, how to read the lounge diagram, and practical advice for the ride. The companion route, station and live-tracker page is Southwest Chief.

The diagram and table below show a typical standard consist, not a guaranteed makeup for your date. Amtrak changes the number of sleepers and coaches with demand, season and shop space. Wikipedia describes “3 or 4 sleeper cars” and “3 or 4 coaches”; rider and industry reports more often count a transition sleeper plus two standard sleepers, and two to three coaches, with shorter winter sets and longer peak-season sets. Private or deadhead cars sometimes ride along. Check your ticket’s car number and, if you can, a recent consist report before you travel.

Typical consist

Cars are listed from the locomotive end (the front of the moving train) toward the rear. After the set is turned at Chicago or Los Angeles, the locomotives are always placed on the leading end; the passenger-car order behind them stays the same.

Industry reporting in early 2026 is a useful reminder that the consist is seasonal. Trains (Bob Johnston, 6 March 2026) described the Chief after the New Year holiday running with a single standard sleeping car plus roomettes in the transition sleeper, then adding another full sleeper and a full baggage car later in March, and a third coach in April after the Winter Park Express season. A photographed eastbound departure from Los Angeles on 13 January 2026 had two P42 locomotives, a transition sleeper, one standard sleeper, a diner, a Sightseer Lounge, one coach and a coach-baggage — a short winter set. Use “typically,” and treat any one day’s count as a snapshot.

How to understand the layout

How to read the consist

  • Amtrak lists cars from the front of the moving train. Your ticket shows a car number (for example 0330) and a room or seat number. Match the car number on the platform before you board — Superliner vestibules are on the lower level.
  • On this train, sleeper car line 330 is typically the sleeping car next to the diner; 331 is one car farther toward the locomotives; the transition sleeper, when it is in the consist, is often 332 (432 on some eastbound reports). These line numbers are a common pattern from rider reports, not a published Amtrak rule, and they can change if a car is added.
  • Every Superliner passenger car is bilevel. You board on the lower level and take a centre stair to the upper level, where most seats and rooms are. Hallways connect cars on the upper level, so once you are upstairs you can walk to the diner and the Sightseer Lounge without going back down — except into the single-level baggage car, which is why the transition sleeper exists.
  • First class is the block of sleeping cars between the baggage/transdorm and the diner. Coach is the block behind the Sightseer Lounge. The dining car and lounge sit between those two blocks and are used by both.

How to read the Sightseer Lounge diagram

The upper-level plan at the top of the first picture is a simple floor plan of one car, not the whole train. Walk it from left to right (or the other way if that end of the car is toward you):

  • Observation area / swivel seats. One end of the upper level is a cluster of rotating chairs aimed at the wraparound windows. This is the classic “front row” for scenery.
  • Lounge area, bar and stairs. In the middle of the car a smaller lounge sits around a bar. The stairs down to the lower level (café, more tables, a toilet) are marked on the plan as “stairs (down).”
  • Table seating. The other end is booths and tables — useful if you want a surface for a map, a laptop or food from the café. The photograph under the plan is taken in this kind of table area, looking along the aisle.
  • TV monitor. A screen is marked at the table-seating end of the plan. It is not a substitute for the windows.
  • Lower level (not drawn on this upper-level plan). The café counter, more seating and a bathroom are downstairs. Café service is listed for all customers on this route when the café is open; it typically closes overnight and sometimes during meal periods. Amtrak Traditional Dining / café note; check official site

A standard Superliner sleeper is a different diagram: five bedrooms and ten roomettes upstairs (bedrooms along one side, roomettes on both sides of a centre aisle), stairs in the middle, and on the lower level a family bedroom at one end, an accessible bedroom at the other, four more roomettes, toilets, a shower and luggage racks. Two bedrooms can be sold together as a bedroom suite. Wikipedia Superliner

Advice

Coach versus sleeper

Coach is a reserved reclining seat, upper or lower level, for the whole trip — two nights if you ride end to end. A sleeper (first class) is a private room, with beds at night, a car attendant, and meals in the dining car included in the fare. Roomettes share toilets and a shower; bedrooms have a toilet and shower in the room. The Family and Accessible bedrooms are on the lower level. Two nights in a seat is a long haul; price a roomette against coach plus the cost of food for your date, and book early — rooms sell out. Fares are not collected here; check Amtrak.com

Best side for scenery

There is no single perfect side, which is why the Sightseer Lounge (windows on both sides) is the place to be for the highlights. Westbound, many regular riders prefer the left (north) side for the approach to Raton Pass, the Sangre de Cristo range and Sandia Peak coming into Albuquerque; the right side is better for some station settings (Raton, Lamy) and a Santa Fe Trail sign on a curve before the Raton tunnel. After Albuquerque, red cliffs toward Gallup are often described on the right (north) westbound. Eastbound, those sides swap. The companion route page notes that westbound train 3 is timed so Raton Pass and the New Mexico desert are in daylight all year. Arrive in the lounge before Trinidad heading west — the car fills.

Meals

Traditional Dining is offered on the Southwest Chief. Meals in the dining car (and room service) are included with a private-room fare. Café service in the Sightseer Lounge is available to all passengers when it is open; café purchases are not included in a sleeper fare. Coach passengers are sometimes offered dining-car seats if space remains after sleeper reservations — this is not guaranteed, and prices change. Reserve lunch and dinner as the crew directs. Amtrak’s published Traditional Dining hours are breakfast 6:30–9:30 am, lunch 11:30 am–2:30 pm and dinner 5:00–9:00 pm, with last call 15 minutes before the end of each period; delays can shift that. Confirm on Amtrak’s dining page and with the crew

When to use the lounge

The Sightseer Lounge is open to coach and sleeper passengers; seats are not reserved. Use it as a shared living room, especially for Raton Pass and the New Mexico stretch, and for both-sides views if your room or seat faces the “wrong” way. It is also the walk-through to the diner from coach. Bring the café downstairs if you want a snack at your seat. It is popular at the scenic hours — go early, and leave bags in your car so you are not occupying a table with luggage.

Boarding and car numbers

Your ticket or app shows the car number and room or seat. At Chicago Union Station and Los Angeles Union Station, follow the posted car numbers on the platform; sleeper passengers can use the Metropolitan Lounge where it is offered (check official site). Board the lower-level door of your own car when you can — walking through many cars with luggage is slow. If you are in 330 you are typically next to the diner; in 331 you walk through 330 to reach it.

Luggage

Checked baggage is available at selected stations only, not at every stop on this route — confirm your origin and destination on Amtrak’s checked-baggage pages before you rely on it. In coach, use the overhead racks (upper level) and the lower-level luggage space. Sleeper rooms have limited space; larger bags often sit on the lower-level luggage racks in your sleeping car. Keep medicines, documents and a night bag with you. Trainside checked bicycles are listed as an accommodation on this route where the station supports them. Size and fee rules: check official site
