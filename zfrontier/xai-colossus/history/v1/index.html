Overview

Colossus is the name used for a cluster of AI training and inference data centers developed by xAI in the Memphis–Southaven area. SpaceX acquired xAI in February 2026; later company materials also use SpaceXAI or SpaceXSI. In SpaceX’s Form 424B4 prospectus dated 11 June 2026 and filed 12 June 2026, COLOSSUS is defined as the flagship data center on Paul R. Lowry Road in Memphis, Tennessee, and COLOSSUS II as data centers in Memphis, Tennessee, and Southaven, Mississippi, “part of our coherent gigawatt-scale AI training cluster.”

The first campus occupies a former Electrolux factory in southwest Memphis, in the Frank C. Pidgeon Industrial Park, beside the historically Black neighborhood of Boxtown. The Greater Memphis Chamber announced the project on 5 June 2024 as xAI’s “Gigafactory of Compute,” to occupy a former manufacturing facility, pending approvals by EDGE, TVA and other authorities. NVIDIA and xAI later described an initial cluster of 100,000 NVIDIA Hopper GPUs, assembled in 122 days, with NVIDIA Spectrum-X Ethernet rather than InfiniBand. Capacity was later expanded. In May 2026 Anthropic announced an agreement to use all compute capacity at Colossus 1, which it described as more than 300 MW and over 220,000 NVIDIA GPUs.

A second campus grew from 2025 around Tulane Road in Whitehaven, Memphis, with additional named buildings and a dedicated generating plant across the state line in Southaven, Mississippi. SpaceX’s June 2026 filing said Colossus and Colossus II together provided approximately 1.0 gigawatt of compute power. Later Musk posts in September and October 2026 described further GB300 deployments and two additional Southaven sites nicknamed Gun Range and Spam. Those later GPU totals are company claims. Data Center Fact Check, writing on 22 November 2026 about the June filing, stated that no independent processor count exists for Colossus 2. Epoch AI’s public Colossus 2 estimate uses satellite imagery last dated 15 June 2026.

How to read the numbers. “Compute power” in the SpaceX filing is not the same as permitted turbine capacity, requested grid supply, or a site’s total electricity use. Memphis Light, Gas and Water (MLGW) reported a 150 MW grid supply to Colossus 1 on a date in May 2025; the requested grid capacity is 300 MW; a Shelby County permit covers 15 turbines totaling 247.2 MW of generating capacity; Epoch AI estimates about 340 MW of IT power at Colossus 1 from a cooling and chip model. Those figures should not be added or treated as interchangeable. No published measurement of actual electricity use at either campus was found for this page.

Networking fabric

The only fabric described in vendor technical detail is the original Colossus (Hopper) cluster.

On 28 October 2024, NVIDIA said Colossus used the NVIDIA Spectrum-X Ethernet platform for its RDMA network, rather than InfiniBand, with Spectrum SN5600 switches (port speeds up to 800 Gb/s, Spectrum-4 ASIC) and BlueField-3 SuperNICs. NVIDIA claimed that, while training a large Grok model, the system had zero application latency degradation or packet loss due to flow collisions across all three tiers of the fabric, and maintained 95% data throughput enabled by Spectrum-X congestion control, compared with 60% that it attributes to standard Ethernet at this scale. Those performance figures are NVIDIA’s claims. Features named in the same release include adaptive routing with NVIDIA Direct Data Placement, congestion control, and enhanced fabric visibility and performance isolation.

The Register (29 Oct 2024), summarizing the same announcement, reported that individual nodes used BlueField-3 SuperNICs with a single 400GbE connection to each GPU, and that ConnectX-8 SuperNICs were described as the path to 800 Gbps per accelerator — a future product point at the time, not a statement that Colossus already used ConnectX-8.

ServeTheHome (28 Oct 2024), from a walkthrough of the cluster, reported a 400GbE or faster optical plant, nine of those links per GPU compute server (about 3.6 Tbps per server in STH’s wording), a dedicated GPU RDMA fabric using BlueField-3 SuperNICs and Spectrum-X, and a separate 400GbE fabric for CPUs, plus lower-speed management networks. STH also noted large, labeled single-mode and multi-mode fiber runs and wrote that liquid-cooled network switches were “desperately needed.”

Supermicro documents 4U liquid-cooled servers with NVIDIA Hopper GPUs, using cold plates, coolant distribution manifolds and coolant distribution units, connected with Spectrum-X. Converge Digest notes Musk’s June 2024 statement that Dell was assembling half the racks for the initial supercomputer and Supermicro the other half, and cautions that this split should not be assumed for later buildings. Switch models, NIC models and topology for Colossus 2, Gun Range and Spam were not identified in Musk’s October 2026 posts, as Converge Digest also notes. Musk, as reported there, said Spam’s inference design needs simpler networking and less than half the fiber of the training configurations — a company comparison, not a measured plant inventory.

Community, environment and permits

Boxtown is a historically Black neighborhood in southwest Memphis, near the first campus. SELC’s 17 June 2025 release said the data center sits near communities already overburdened by industrial sources, and that studies show Boxtown faces a cancer risk four times the national average — SELC’s citation, not a figure recalculated here. Residents and groups including the NAACP, Young, Gifted & Green, and Protect Our Aquifer have objected to unpermitted or temporary turbines, air emissions (including NOx and formaldehyde, as alleged in later filings reported by DCD), and continued use of the Memphis Sand aquifer for cooling until a recycling plant is finished.

Whether the first turbines needed a permit was disputed. A consultant for xAI said temporary turbines did not, for their first year, a view NBC News reported the mayor, the health department and the chamber endorsed (as cited by Footprint). SELC senior attorney Amanda Garcia called not getting a permit from day one “circumvention.” xAI said its operations “comply with all applicable laws.” After the 2 July 2025 permit, the company promised emissions controls that would make the site “the lowest emitting of its kind in the country.” The county board dismissed the NAACP / Young, Gifted & Green appeal as moot on 15 December 2025. Footprint had not found a lawsuit over the Memphis turbines themselves as of 5 November 2026; the NAACP did sue in April 2026 over the Southaven plant.

In Mississippi, DCD (2 Nov 2026) reported a stormwater-permit notice with preliminary claimed exposure of approximately $6.1 million across three sites, and that SpaceXAI said it was cooperating and in discussions to resolve matters through a single agreed order. The same article said a judge had rejected a Department of Justice attempt to block the NAACP lawsuit, and that the NAACP alleged 57 gas turbines at Southaven. An evidentiary hearing on the issued Southaven permit, noted in Fact Check’s project record as set for 9 November 2026, has an outcome that was not collected for this page.

On water, the City of Memphis has hosted both a SpaceXAI fact sheet (17 Aug 2026) and the 14 September 2026 letter reaffirming the CWRP, with a no-later-than January 2027 restart. FOX13 reported skepticism in Boxtown after the pause, and Protect Our Aquifer’s director saying in June that there was still no written binding agreement. Those two tracks — a company recommitment on a city letterhead host, and advocates asking for an enforceable deadline — were both still on the record as of the sources opened for this page.

On the benefit side, Fact Check records that Colossus 1 pays full property taxes and that on 19 August 2025 the Memphis City Council voted to steer part of that revenue to the surrounding area, and that no tax break or incentive agreement for either campus appeared in the records it read. Colossus 2 tax bills were not published in those records.
