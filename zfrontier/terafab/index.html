💠 Terafab

Terafab is the name Elon Musk gave to a planned chip-making effort for Tesla, SpaceX and xAI. The idea is to design and manufacture enough AI chips for vehicles, Optimus robots and space-based data centres, instead of relying only on foundry partners. This page is a sourced snapshot as of 2026-11-13. It is not a daily tracker, and it does not invent missing figures.

As of 2026-11-13. The research fab at Giga Texas is under construction. The large production plant is planned for Grimes County, Texas. Volume production is an aspiration for around the third quarter of 2028, with Musk himself putting "big error bars" on that date. Binding financial terms between the companies were still not fully set in SpaceX's May 2026 IPO filing. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

What it is, and why they want it

Musk first sketched a "Tesla TeraFab" at Tesla's annual shareholder meeting on 6 November 2025. He said even a best-case outlook from Tesla's foundry partners would not be enough, and that Tesla would probably have to build "a gigantic chip fab." He compared the idea to a Gigafactory, "but way bigger." [7] [8] [9]

The formal Terafab announcement came on 21-22 March 2026 in Austin, as a collaboration of Tesla, SpaceX and xAI. Musk's short explanation, quoted by TechCrunch, was: "We either build the Terafab or we don't have the chips, and we need the chips, so we build the Terafab." [10] [11] [12] [13]

On X the next day he wrote, as quoted by Fox Business and Just Auto, that "Terafab will technically be two fabs, each making only one chip design." One design is for Tesla vehicles and Optimus robots. The other is a high-power chip for SpaceX space-based data centres, which he said has to tolerate a harsher radiation environment and run a little hotter than a typical Earth chip so that radiator mass can be kept down. [11] [12]

At the March event he thanked existing suppliers, naming Samsung, TSMC and Micron, but said demand from his companies would eventually exceed total global chip output. TechCrunch reported a nearer-term goal of chips supporting 100 to 200 gigawatts of computing power a year on Earth, plus a terawatt in space. SpaceX's May 2026 S-1 later defined Terafab as "a chip manufacturing initiative with a long-term goal of producing one terawatt of compute hardware each year." Fox Business also recorded Musk comparing that one-terawatt-a-year aim with about half a terawatt of electricity generation across the United States. [11] [10] [5]

SpaceX's 6 August 2026 update said the factory would combine manufacturing, packaging and testing of advanced logic and memory in one place, and would make edge and inference chips for Optimus and Cybercab as well as high-power chips for space-based data centres. [1]

AI5, AI6 and the foundry partners

Terafab is a long-term plan to make chips in-house. Until it is running at volume, Tesla still depends on contract foundries for the chips it has already designed.

Samsung

In July 2025 Nikkei Asia reported a $16.5 billion Samsung deal to supply chips for Tesla through the end of 2033. At the November 2025 meeting Musk listed Samsung Korea among the sites that would make AI5. In March 2026 he thanked Samsung as a current supplier. That contract is foundry supply. It is not, on the evidence opened for this page, a contract to operate Terafab.[14] [7] [11]

TSMC

At the same meeting Musk said AI5 would also be made at TSMC Taiwan, TSMC Arizona and "TSMC Texas." This page has not found a later official confirmation of a Tesla production line at a TSMC plant in Texas, so that fourth site is recorded as he said it in November 2025. He hoped AI6 could follow in the same fabs less than a year after AI5 started, doubling the performance metrics. In October 2026 he said TSMC might only sublease part of Terafab.[7] [19] [17]

Intel

In November 2025 Musk said "maybe we'll do something with Intel" but that no deal had been signed. Intel said on 7 April 2026 that it had joined Terafab. Reuters reported on 22 April that Musk said Tesla planned to use Intel's 14A process there. How the collaboration works in practice, including volumes and fees, has not been published in the sources opened for this page.[7] [9] [15] [16] [4]

Micron and others

Musk thanked Micron, with Samsung and TSMC, as a current supplier in March 2026. SpaceX later said Terafab would also handle memory, packaging and testing. No Micron contract terms for Terafab were found.[11] [1]

On Tesla's 21 October 2026 call, Musk said Tesla hoped to put the AI5 chip into Optimus around April 2027, and described AI5 as about an order of magnitude more compute than AI4. That is a Tesla chip-integration target, not a statement that Terafab will be the factory making those first AI5 parts. The sources opened for this page do not name the first useful chip generation Terafab itself will produce at volume. [3]

At the 2025 shareholder meeting Musk also said Tesla believed AI5 would use about a third of the power of an Nvidia Blackwell, for roughly comparable performance, and "much less than ten percent of the cost," because the chip is specialised for Tesla's own integer-heavy inference stack rather than a general-purpose GPU. Those are Musk's comparisons, not third-party measurements. [7]

Locations and scale

The March 2026 launch placed Terafab in Austin, near Tesla's headquarters and Gigafactory. SpaceX's 6 August 2026 update then split the plan: a research fab already started on the North Campus of Giga Texas, and a production Terafab in Grimes County, near Brazos County. Tesla's Q3 2026 update confirms that construction of "the Austin Fab" was under way, with equipment commissioning planned in 2027. [10] [1] [2]

Figures that have been stated, and those that have not:

  • Planned floor space. SpaceX said a vertically integrated factory with more than 100 million square feet of manufacturing space is planned. That is a planned figure, not a built area. [1]
  • Initial capital. SpaceX estimated the initial phase at approximately $16.8 billion from SpaceX and Tesla, "with potential future expansion phases bringing total investment much higher." [1]
  • Long-term capital. On 21 October 2026 Musk said equipment through early 2028 would be "not less than tens of billions," and the project would "probably be north of $100 billion long term." Tesla's finance chief, Vaibhav Taneja, said most research-fab equipment orders were already placed. SpaceX's 12 November 2026 update called it "the $100B Terafab advanced chip manufacturing project." The split between Tesla and SpaceX had not been decided on the October call. [3] [6]
  • Jobs. SpaceX said the Grimes County facility would employ at least 3,000 people, many from Grimes and nearby Brazos County, and pointed to a 60% to 80% local-hiring pattern at its other Texas sites. [1]
  • Water. SpaceX said the site would use water from Gibbons Creek Reservoir for industrial operations rather than local groundwater, with on-site wastewater treatment and reuse. [1]
  • Wafer starts. The 100,000 and 1 million wafer-starts-per-month figures come from Musk at the 6 November 2025 meeting. SpaceX's later official update did not repeat a wafer-start number. This page has not found a later official revision of those rates. [7] [8] [1]
  • Exact parcel and acreage in Grimes County were not given in the sources opened for this page.

How that compares with existing fabs

TSMC's 2024 annual report said its manufacturing facilities had annual production capacity of approximately 17 million 12-inch equivalent wafers. Dividing that annual figure by 12 months gives about 1.42 million wafer starts a month. CNBC used the same annual figure, and the same monthly conversion, when it compared TSMC with Musk's November 2025 scale comments. [20] [8]

If Terafab ever reached Musk's long-term 1 million wafer starts a month, that would be in the same order of magnitude as TSMC's entire 2024 capacity. A first 100,000 wafer-start fab would be about 7% of that 2024 TSMC monthly figure (100,000 / 1.42 million). Both percentages are derived from those two published numbers; they are not a TSMC or Tesla forecast, and they assume comparable wafer-size counting. [7] [8] [20]

Reuters, reporting the 20 October 2026 SpaceX call, noted that cutting-edge logic factories in the United States can take three or more years to build, which is why Musk's Q3 2028 volume date is unusually fast if it is met. Tesla's own 2026 capex guidance was revised to about $20 billion on that October call, a much smaller annual number than Musk's long-term Terafab bill, which is why the still-undecided split with SpaceX matters. [4] [3]

In mid-March 2026, just before the Austin announcement, Sherwood News reported a Morgan Stanley estimate that Terafab could cost an extra $35 billion to $45 billion of capex, including about $20 billion to $25 billion for wafer-fabrication equipment. That was an analyst estimate, dated 17 March 2026, and it predates SpaceX's $16.8 billion initial-phase figure and Musk's later "north of $100 billion long term" comment. It is included here only as an early third-party range, not as a company figure. [21]

Risks and open questions

Several official documents and Musk's own comments already flag that this is an aggressive plan, not a finished factory. The list below sticks to those points and to facts that were not found.

  1. The timetable is explicitly uncertain. On 20 October 2026 Musk said "I put big error bars on this because we don't know if we will actually achieve this or not," called the dates "very aggressive," and said volume production in Q3 or Q4 of 2028 was "at least in the set of possible outcomes." Reuters noted his history of missed timelines. [4] [3]
  2. The legal deal was still a framework in May 2026. SpaceX's S-1, filed 20 May 2026, said specific Terafab projects, "including any development timelines, milestones and capital expenditures," had "not yet been determined." Electrek, reading the same filing, reported further risk-factor language that the project was in "very early stages," that financial terms, intellectual-property rights and the term of the collaboration had not been finalised, and that neither Tesla nor Intel was obligated to remain in the project. [5] [18]
  3. Who pays, and how much, is still open. Musk said the Tesla-SpaceX split had to be figured out. No official line-item for Terafab sat inside Tesla's about-$20 billion 2026 capex guide. [3]
  4. Equipment is a bottleneck. Leading-edge tools, especially EUV lithography from ASML, have long lead times. Musk thanked ASML and other suppliers for accelerating deliveries but did not, in the Reuters report, directly answer whether EUV machines had been ordered. [4]
  5. Process and control are unsettled. Intel's 14A role was announced, but contract detail was not. A TSMC operating role was later ruled out by Musk, except possibly as a subtenant, which leaves an unresolved question of who actually runs which cleanroom. [16] [17] [19]
  6. Semiconductor manufacturing is new territory for these companies. TechCrunch, citing Bloomberg, noted that Musk does not have a semiconductor-manufacturing background and has a record of over-promising on goals and timelines. [10]
  7. Several basic product facts are still unpublished in the sources opened for this page: the first chip generation Terafab will make at volume; a later official wafer-start target for Grimes County; a detailed site plan or acreage; and any Micron or memory-partner contract for the on-site memory work SpaceX described.

None of that means the project has been cancelled. Tesla is building the Austin research fab, SpaceX has named Grimes County for the production plant, and both companies have repeated the 2027-2028 research and volume targets. It does mean the public record, as of 2026-11-13, is still a plan with large uncertainties.
