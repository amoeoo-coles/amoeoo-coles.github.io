MWMESHAustralia plan
Australia plan

Join or build a similar mesh around Melbourne

MountainWest Mesh is a US community running MeshCore in the 915 MHz band. A similar off-grid mesh in Melbourne or elsewhere in Australia is legally a different radio plan: the band is 915–928 MHz under the ACMA LIPD class licence, and you should join an existing Australian community’s settings rather than copying MWMESH’s 910.525 MHz US frequency. As of 13 Nov 2026 (Melbourne time).

ACMA rules for the LoRa band

In Australia, short-range devices including those in 915–928 MHz can operate under the Radiocommunications (Low Interference Potential Devices) Class Licence 2025. You do not apply for this licence or pay a fee, but you must follow the instrument and the Radiocommunications Act 1992. Class-licence users get no protection from interference, and devices must not cause interference to other radiocommunication services [1] [2].

The 2025 instrument commenced at the start of 1 October 2025 (authorised version F2025L01047). For 915–928 MHz, Schedule 1 includes these official entries [2]:

Class of transmitterMaximum EIRPAdditional limitation
Any transmitter3 mWNone (Schedule 1, item for 915–928 MHz at 3 mW)
Frequency hopping radiocommunications transmitters1 WClause 39: must not use fewer than 20 hopping frequencies
Digital modulation radiocommunications transmitters1 WClause 43: radiated peak power spectral density must not be greater than 25 mW per 3 kHz

Which item a given LoRa radio falls under depends on how that transmitter is classified, not on a label in this page. The 1 W figures are official maxima with those extra limits, not a target to run at. Antenna gain counts toward EIRP. This page does not calculate a device’s power spectral density. Read the current instrument before transmitting [2] [1].

The ACMA page also states extra rules near the Murchison Radioastronomy Observatory in central Western Australia, including an exclusion zone of 70 km around the observatory [1].

MeshCore and Meshtastic region settings (not a single “ANZ” for both)

MeshCore does not use one “ANZ” radio preset. Official MeshCore docs say suggested presets live on the MeshCore App API and are community suggestions; you may set any legal parameters [3]. The API on 13 Nov 2026 listed these Australia and New Zealand entries (all Australia entries below also suggest a 2-byte path hash) [4]:

Use the preset your local community already runs. Two nodes on different presets will not hear each other. This page does not invent a Melbourne-only frequency [3] [5].

Meshtastic does have a region code ANZ for Australia and New Zealand: 915.0–928.0 MHz, with a firmware power ceiling of 30 dBm and a 100% duty-cycle entry in that table. Official Meshtastic docs say Australia should use ANZ, and that the listed power limit is the firmware ceiling, not your national limit — follow the ACMA licence [6] [7].

Existing Australian and Victorian communities

These official community sites were found on 13 Nov 2026. Join them and ask what is on air near you, rather than starting a private incompatible frequency.

  • eastmesh.au — “Australian MeshCore Community Network”. It describes MeshCore as a secure, text-based LoRa system and supports community mesh infrastructure [5]. A Victoria-oriented EastMesh analyzer is at vic.eastmesh.au [8].
  • NSW Mesh — a New South Wales community. Its getting-started page says most local activity has shifted to MeshCore and that MeshCore is recommended for most participants, while Meshtastic remains welcome for small groups, portable use and experimentation [9].
  • Meshtastic Australia — a community site for running Meshtastic in Australia, pointing back to Meshtastic.org for the project itself [10].

No official, Melbourne-only mesh homepage with a published node list was used as a source here, so this page does not invent a Victorian node count or coverage map.

Antenna and site tips

Only guidance that official MWMESH or MeshCore pages actually state:

  • Low-power LoRa can travel miles with good placement and line of sight [20].
  • Place repeaters at high elevations or other high locations for coverage; MeshCore highlights solar-powered repeaters in high locations [21] [19].
  • The official FAQ says: if you have a spare device and neighbours on the mesh, flash it as a repeater and place it high above ground [13].
  • MWMESH’s handheld power recommendation is 22 dBm, and it tells operators to account for antenna gain so EIRP stays within the (US) 30 dBm figure they cite. In Australia, stay within the LIPD EIRP and spectral-density limits instead [22] [2].

This page does not invent antenna gains, ranges in kilometres, or mast heights.

Phased plan

Phase 0 — Research

Read the LIPD class licence, find EastMesh / NSW Mesh / Meshtastic Australia, and ask which firmware and preset is already in use near Melbourne. Do not start by copying US MWMESH radio settings.

Phase 1 — One companion, correctly set

Obtain a supported 915 MHz companion, flash official firmware, install the app, and apply the local community preset (or Meshtastic region ANZ if that is the group you are joining).

Phase 2 — Talk on the existing mesh

Set a short name, send a test direct message, and join only the published local channel or room. Keep messages short.

Phase 3 — Optional coverage

Improve height and line of sight first. Add a dedicated repeater only when the local community would benefit, with the correct preset, a changed admin password, and EIRP inside the class licence. Share a map pin only if you accept publishing an approximate location.
