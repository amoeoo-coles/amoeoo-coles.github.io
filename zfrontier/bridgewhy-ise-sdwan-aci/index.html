How they integrate

The three BridgeWhy course pages treat ISE, SD-WAN and ACI as separate courses. They do not describe a shared policy domain. The integration picture below comes from the Common Policy page on this site, which summarises Cisco’s Common Policy Integration Guide, and from the Cisco TrustSec and ISE documents in References. “Covered in the three BridgeWhy courses” for this pairing is not collected.

In that Common Policy model, Cisco ISE is the policy hub. It classifies users and workloads, normalises them as Security Group Tags (SGTs), and distributes IP-to-SGT bindings:

  • pxGrid publishes context to platforms such as APIC, SD-WAN Manager and Secure Firewall Management Center.
  • SXP (SGT Exchange Protocol) carries the same bindings to TrustSec listeners that do not read inline tags, including some SD-WAN edges and campus enforcement points.
  • On the ACI side, the ISE ACI connector can map EPGs (and, in the Cisco guide, ESGs) to SGTs, and campus or WAN SGTs can return to APIC as external groups so ACI contracts can enforce them on the leaf.

See the original three-domain drawing in section 5, and the full validated design on the Common Policy page, including topology, enforcement points and a bill of materials.

ESG, pxGrid, SXP, SGT and the ISE ACI connector are Cisco Common Policy / TrustSec terms. None of those strings appear on the ISE, SD-WAN or ACI BridgeWhy course pages used as the course source for this comparison.

Suggested learning order

This order follows the six-phase progression on the BridgeWhy hub. It is not a BridgeWhy marketing claim beyond what that page already says.

  1. Phases 1–3 first, if you are following the full curriculum. The hub puts mindset and CCNA-level foundations, packet analysis and IPv6, then interior and exterior routing, WAN and QoS, before Phase 4. Those earlier courses are out of scope for this page.
  2. Phase 4 — take the ACI and SD-WAN courses in this phase. The hub lists both under “Enterprise Campus, Data Center, SD-WAN & Wireless”. It does not say which of the two to take first; a prescribed sequence between ACI and SD-WAN is not collected. The ACI package already includes the recorded VXLAN with BGP EVPN videos, and Lesson 3 of ACI from Scratch assumes you may already know VXLAN with BGP-EVPN, so those recorded videos are a natural partner to the ACI course rather than a substitute for SD-WAN.
  3. Phase 5 — take ISE after the two Phase 4 courses. The hub places ISE under “Network Security & Zero Trust Access”, after the data-center and SD-WAN phase. That order also matches the Common Policy idea that ISE becomes useful as a hub once there is a campus or WAN edge and an ACI fabric to receive SGT context — a pairing the ISE course page itself does not describe.
  4. Then read the Common Policy page at /zfrontier/common-policy/ to see how ISE, ACI and SD-WAN are designed to work as one policy domain. That page is a Cisco validated-profile summary, not a fourth BridgeWhy course.
